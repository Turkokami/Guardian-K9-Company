## Why is the schedule built around the north?

Because that is where we are, and because the north has historically been served from
somewhere else.

Canine detection in Washington is overwhelmingly organised out of the Puget Sound corridor.
For a property in Bellingham, Mount Vernon or Lynden, that has meant being covered as the
far end of somebody else's route. The predictable results are long lead times, dates that
move when a closer job appears, and a quiet reluctance that nobody says out loud.

We inverted it. Whatcom and Skagit are the prime work zones. Every incorporated city and
town in Skagit County sits inside the service area, and so does every incorporated city in
Whatcom. That is a deliberate commitment to a region rather than a claim of statewide
uniformity, and it means the north gets first call on the calendar instead of last.

The trade is honest and worth stating: a property in Vancouver or Spokane gets a worse
response time from us than it would from a company based nearby. We will still come. We just
will not pretend the distance is not there.

## What does "service-area business" mean for you?

We come to the property, and we publish no street address.

Guardian works at your site. There is no shop, no counter and nothing to visit, so there is
no address on this site, in our schema markup or on our business listings. That is a
deliberate and standard arrangement for a mobile trade rather than an evasion, and it is why
every contact route here is a phone, email or form rather than a map pin.

Practically, it means scoping happens through description rather than a walk-in
conversation. Tell us the property type, the unit count or acreage, the construction, the
access situation and what prompted the call, and we can usually scope accurately without a
site visit first. For larger commercial programmes we will often want to walk the property
before quoting, and that visit is arranged like any other.

## How do the northern counties differ from each other?

Enough that the pages are genuinely different documents rather than one template with the
names swapped.

**Whatcom** is anchored by [Bellingham](/service-area/bellingham/), the largest rental
market north of Everett, where a university calendar drives turnover and the city runs a
rental registration and safety inspection programme that has made owners comfortable with
third-party inspection. Around it, the county diverges sharply.
[Ferndale](/service-area/ferndale/) runs on Cherry Point industrial employment and the
contractor lodging that spikes around plant turnarounds.
[Lynden](/service-area/lynden/) sits in the agricultural belt, where seasonal labour housing
is a distinct inspection setting with its own licensing and access rules.
[Blaine](/service-area/blaine/) carries two border crossings and a vacation-rental base at
Semiahmoo and Birch Bay. And [Everson](/service-area/everson/),
[Nooksack](/service-area/nooksack/) and [Sumas](/service-area/sumas/) are still shaped by
the November 2021 Nooksack flooding, which damaged the overwhelming majority of structures
in Sumas and displaced hundreds of people across the north county. Displacement, temporary
housing and replacement furniture are documented introduction pathways, and that is written
about honestly on those pages rather than generalised.

**Skagit** is led by [Mount Vernon](/service-area/mount-vernon/), the county seat, which
holds the courts, the county offices and the county's main concentration of conventional
apartment stock. [Burlington](/service-area/burlington/) carries the I-5 retail and lodging
cluster, so transient accommodation concentrates there rather than where the residents live.
[Anacortes](/service-area/anacortes/) is an island city running on two March Point refineries
and a state ferry terminal, which produces both scheduled contractor surges and seasonal
visitor flow. [Sedro-Woolley](/service-area/sedro-woolley/) has mill-era wood-frame stock
that genuinely takes longer to search than anything modern.
[La Conner](/service-area/la-conner/) is small historic inns and restoration-constrained
building fabric. [Concrete](/service-area/concrete/) is upper valley, where travel time is a
constraint we state rather than absorb.

**Island County** enters through [Oak Harbor](/service-area/oak-harbor/), reached across
Deception Pass. Naval Air Station Whidbey Island drives a rental market that turns over on
posting orders rather than on an academic or calendar cycle, and military households move
with goods packed, shipped and stored by third parties.

## What about the towns without their own pages?

They are covered on the same terms. They just do not get a page written to look thorough.

Hamilton and Lyman are very small upper-valley towns on the Skagit River floodplain. We
serve them. What we will not do is generate a three-thousand-word page about each, because
we cannot fill one with real local research, and a page that reads as a template is worse
for you and for us than an honest line on this page. That standard applies to us as much as
to anybody: the site's own build process refuses to index a page that has not earned it.

If you own property in a town that is not named anywhere on this site, the answer is still
almost certainly yes. Ask.

## How does island and ferry work get scheduled?

Around the timetable, always in advance, and never promised same-day.

Anacortes is the mainland gateway to the San Juans and a named service area in its own right.
Oak Harbor and the Whidbey corridor come across Deception Pass by road. Work that depends on
a sailing has a hard constraint that no amount of goodwill removes: if the boat is full or
the schedule changes, the appointment changes with it.

So island work is scheduled with margin. We would rather book a date a week out that holds
than offer a date in two days that we then have to move. For multi-property island work we
will normally group the sites into a single trip, which is both cheaper for you and more
reliable.

## What does a multi-site portfolio look like?

One schedule, one document format, and a file that accumulates in the same shape across
every property.

The value of a portfolio programme is not primarily the per-visit price. It is that a
regional manager holding twelve buildings across three counties ends up with twelve records
in one format, on one cadence, rather than twelve differently structured PDFs from four
vendors on no cadence at all. When a dispute arrives at one property, the surrounding
documentation is what makes the response fast.

That is straightforward to deliver across Whatcom and Skagit, workable through the northern
corridor to Everett, and genuinely harder the further south a portfolio spreads. We will say
which of those three your portfolio is when we scope it. The programme structure itself is
set out at [multifamily and property management](/commercial/multifamily/).

## How far south and east do you actually go?

All the way, on a booked date, and the honest picture differs by market.

**Everett** is the hinge. Snohomish County is close enough to the northern corridor that it
sits on a normal schedule rather than being a special trip, which is why it retains full
depth in our service area. Naval Station and aerospace shift patterns there make
daytime-only inspection scheduling unworkable, so it tends to be evening and early work.

**Seattle** is a real service area and a scheduled one. It is also the market with the most
specific rules: bed bugs are named explicitly in the municipal code, penalties run from $150
to $500 per day, and the rental registration and inspection ordinance subjects registered
rentals to periodic inspection. A portfolio spanning Seattle and the north needs two
documentation standards, not one, and that is worth knowing before a policy is written.

**Bellevue, Tacoma, Kent, Renton and Olympia** each carry a city page and are covered on a
booked basis. They no longer carry the per-service child pages the northern markets do, and
that is a deliberate reflection of where the schedule actually sits rather than an editing
oversight.

**Spokane** is the opposite corner of the state and is planned travel, full stop. It is also
genuinely different work: eastern Washington's continental climate produces a different
indoor pest season from the maritime west side, and reusing west-side seasonal assumptions
there would be wrong.

**Vancouver** in Clark County is the furthest point in the service area from the northern
base. It has a particular quirk worth flagging: renters and landlords there consume Portland
media but are governed by Washington law, so RCW 59.18 applies and Oregon statute does not.
That confusion is real and largely unserved by anybody.

For all of these the arrangement is the same. We will come, the date is agreed in advance,
and travel is a factor in scoping rather than a surprise added afterwards.

## What actually determines a response time?

Four things, and only one of them is distance.

**Where the work sits relative to the schedule.** A Whatcom or Skagit property slots into an
existing week. A Spokane property requires a trip to be built around it, which means it
needs either enough work to justify the journey or a date far enough out to combine with
something else.

**Whether it is one unit or a building.** Single-property work is usually quick to place.
Building-wide work is constrained by your notice obligations rather than by our calendar,
because entry notice has to be served properly under RCW 59.18.150 before we arrive. A sweep
booked for next Tuesday that cannot have notices served by Monday is not a sweep.

**Access complexity.** Sites where several parties control different spaces, or where a
tribal government, a public body or a separate housing authority is involved, take longer to
arrange and are worth starting early.

**Ferries and geography.** Island work is timetable-dependent, and upper-valley work in
places like Concrete carries real drive time from the I-5 corridor.

We would rather give you a date that holds than the earliest date that sounds good. If you
need something urgently, say so when you call: sometimes the schedule flexes, and when it
genuinely cannot we will tell you that instead of booking you and moving it later.

## Whose permission does the work sometimes need?

Not always the person who called us, and getting this wrong wastes a day.

**Sovereign tribal governments.** Several of our named service areas neighbour sovereign
nations, including the Lummi Nation near Ferndale and the Swinomish Indian Tribal Community
beside La Conner. These are governments with their own housing authorities, their own
procurement and their own jurisdiction. County or city process does not carry across, and it
would be wrong to assume otherwise. Work on tribal land is arranged with that government.

**Public land managers.** Goose work on municipal parkland requires the parks department or
land manager to engage us. An adjoining HOA cannot authorise work on ground it does not own,
however much the flock crossing the boundary is genuinely their problem. Where a site
straddles ownership, that needs resolving before the programme starts.

**Landlords versus tenants versus managers.** For rental inspections, the entry notice
obligation under RCW 59.18.150 sits with the landlord, and the person who calls us is not
always the person able to serve it. On agricultural workforce housing the organisation
controlling access is frequently not the owner at all.

**Institutional facilities departments.** Hospitals, colleges and care settings usually have
an internal process that has to be satisfied before an outside vendor walks the building,
and it is normally slower than a private landlord's.

None of this is unusual and none of it is an obstacle. It just needs establishing at the
scoping stage rather than discovered at the door, which is why we ask who controls access
before we ask what date suits you.

## What should you tell us when you get in touch?

Five things, and having them ready turns a vague enquiry into a real quote.

Where the property is, and whether there are several. What type it is and how it is built,
because a pre-war wood-frame building and a 2018 podium block take different amounts of time
per unit. How many units or how much acreage. What prompted the call, including whether
anyone has already inspected or treated. And what your access situation is, meaning who can
actually let us into every space on the list.

With those five we can usually tell you what the work involves, what it would cost and when
we could genuinely be there. If the honest answer is that we are the wrong choice for your
location, or that you do not need us yet, that is what you will hear. It costs us a job and
it is the reason the next call gets returned.

And if your property sits outside every county named on this page, ask anyway. The list
describes where the schedule is densest, not a boundary we refuse to cross. Washington has
thirty-nine counties and we have named nine of them here, which reflects where the work
currently is rather than where it is permitted to go. A property in Chelan, Kitsap, Yakima
or anywhere else is a scheduling question and a travel question, not a refusal.
