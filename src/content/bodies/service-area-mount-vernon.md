## Why Skagit County is a prime work zone

Because we are based in the north and organised around it, which is unusual in this trade.

Canine detection in Washington is mostly run out of the Puget Sound corridor. Skagit gets
covered when somebody is already driving to Bellingham, which means long lead times and a
quiet preference for the job closer to home. Guardian is arranged the other way round.
Whatcom and Skagit are the prime work zones and everything south of Everett is a travel
market, booked in advance rather than promised same-day.

Mount Vernon is the county seat and the largest city in Skagit, which makes it the centre
of gravity for both halves of what we do. It holds the county's main concentration of
conventional apartment stock, its courts and county offices, and a significant
agricultural workforce housing segment that operates on entirely different rules.

## Who is responsible for bed bugs in a Mount Vernon rental?

The landlord, under state law, with two carve-outs that decide most disputes.

**RCW 59.18.060(4)** requires a landlord to provide a reasonable pest control programme at
the initiation of a tenancy and, other than in a single-family residence, to control
infestation during the tenancy except where the infestation is caused by the tenant.
**RCW 59.18.130** sets out tenant duties and is where liability for extermination costs
comes from when the tenant is the cause. **RCW 59.18.150** governs entry notice, which is
the provision that decides whether a building-wide sweep is workable or not.

Mount Vernon adds nothing municipal on top. There is no local provision naming bed bugs
here of the kind Seattle has in SMC 22.206.160.A.3, and no local penalty schedule. State
law is the whole of it.

That cuts both ways. There is less to comply with, and there is also no municipal
inspection finding to point at when causation is argued. What you have is your own record,
which is an argument for keeping a good one rather than for relaxing. The general position
is summarised at
[who is responsible under Washington landlord-tenant law](/compliance/washington-landlord-tenant-bed-bugs/),
and the notice rules at
[entry notice for unit inspections](/compliance/entry-notice-for-unit-inspections/).

## What does the Tulip Festival do to the lodging calendar?

It concentrates a large share of the year's short-stay turnover into a few weeks in April.

The Skagit Valley Tulip Festival runs through April and pulls very heavy visitor volume
into the valley floor. For operators of lodging and vacation rentals, that is not simply a
busy month. It is a period during which the turnover rate rises sharply and the ability to
take a room out of service falls to nearly zero at exactly the same time.

The scheduling consequence is straightforward once you say it out loud. The useful
screening moments are before the season starts and after it ends. Screening in the middle
is when you least want to find something, because the room you would have to hold is the
room you cannot afford to hold. Operators who book a pre-season sweep and a post-season
verification tend to spend less over the year than operators who wait for a complaint in
the second week of April.

The wider setting is written up at [hotels and lodging](/commercial/hotels-and-lodging/),
and [Burlington](/service-area/burlington/) carries more of the county's I-5 lodging
cluster than Mount Vernon does.

## What is different about agricultural workforce housing?

Almost everything, and it is the part of this market most often written about carelessly.

Seasonal agricultural labour housing in Skagit operates under its own licensing, access
and notice arrangements. Occupancy patterns follow the crop calendar rather than a lease
year. The person who controls access is frequently not the person who owns the building.
Residents may not share a first language with the inspector, and the practical
consequences of a positive finding land on people with very little slack in their
circumstances.

None of that makes the work harder to do well. It makes it different to scope. We ask
about access arrangements, about who is authorised to admit us and about language needs
before we ask about dates. A search that is arranged on conventional multifamily
assumptions will arrive to find half the doors shut and will produce a document that
overstates its own coverage.

I will not pretend to expertise I do not have about the licensing framework itself. What I
can say is that we scope this housing separately, we record inconclusive units as
inconclusive rather than clear, and we do not treat a partial search as a clean bill.

## How do the county's institutional buyers differ?

They need the document to survive somebody else reading it.

Mount Vernon holds the Skagit County courthouse and the county offices, and the city also
carries Skagit Valley College and Skagit Valley Hospital. Buyers of this kind procure
differently from a private landlord. There is a specification, there is often a
requirement that the person who did the work signs for it, there is an expectation that
the file can be produced years later, and there is usually somebody in the chain whose job
is to find the weakness in it.

That suits how we already work. Every report is dated and signed by the handler who
performed the inspection. Every alert carries its visual confirmation result. Unconfirmed
alerts stay in the document rather than being quietly removed, because a report in which
every alert was confirmed is a report that should raise an eyebrow. The reasoning is set
out at
[what makes a report defensible](/compliance/what-a-defensible-report-contains/).

## The geese in this valley are not the geese we work on

This is the most important thing on this page, and almost nobody in the trade says it.

Skagit is famous for its winter geese. Those are **snow geese**, from the Wrangel Island
population, arriving in counts that frequently run above fifty thousand across the valley
and the Skagit Wildlife Area, which WDFW manages across roughly 18,138 acres in
twenty-one separate units. The agricultural conflict around them is real and
well-documented, driven in part by a long-run shift in what the valley grows. WDFW's
principal management response has been to encourage hunting, including through a quality
hunt programme run in partnership with local farmers and funded through hunting licence
revenue and a federal public-access programme.

Guardian does not work on that problem. Our goose service is **handler-directed hazing of
resident Canada geese on managed grounds**: no contact, no injury, no nests or eggs
disturbed. Resident Canada geese are a different bird, a different population, a different
legal regime and a different site type. They are the birds on the corporate lawn, the
retention pond, the HOA common area, the school field and the golf course.

If a company offers to send a dog at your snow geese on a Skagit field, ask it a lot of
questions. Canada geese are federally protected under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act and
"take" is defined at 50 CFR 10.12 to include pursuit, so the framing matters legally and
not merely presentationally. WDFW's Living with Wildlife guidance is explicit that
handler-directed dogs are the method of choice for large open areas such as golf courses,
airports, parks, agricultural fields and corporate parks, and equally explicit that dogs
can be inappropriate in residential areas, continuously used parks, near roadways and on
large water bodies. We take both halves seriously.

Two standing limits. We do not offer nest or egg work, because registration under the
Resident Canada Goose Nest and Egg Depredation Order at 50 CFR 21.162 belongs to the
landowner, the association or the local government rather than to a vendor. And hazing
pauses during the flightless molt, because birds that cannot fly cannot be moved lawfully
or humanely. That pause is published operating policy, not a surprise you discover in
July. For completeness, RCW 77.15.240 on unlawful use of dogs covers deer, elk, moose,
caribou, mountain sheep and endangered species; it does not restrict goose hazing.

The full legal picture is at
[Canada goose law in Washington](/compliance/canada-goose-law-washington/), the grounds
setting at [HOAs, golf and grounds](/commercial/hoas-and-grounds/), and the two most
common site problems at
[geese on a retention pond](/services/k9-goose-deterrent/geese-on-a-retention-pond/) and
[droppings and walkway liability](/services/k9-goose-deterrent/droppings-and-walkway-liability/).

## Does the river and the floodwall matter to an inspection?

Indirectly, and more than you would think.

Downtown Mount Vernon sits behind a Skagit River floodwall, and the valley floor has a
long relationship with high water. The relevance to this work is not the water itself. It
is what follows it: remediation, replaced furnishings, temporary accommodation and moves
that nobody planned. Displacement and replacement furniture are documented introduction
pathways, and they arrive on a schedule that no property manager sets.

Elsewhere in the region this factor is much sharper still. The November 2021 Nooksack
flooding in Whatcom County damaged the great majority of structures in Sumas and displaced
hundreds of people, and the honest version of that story is on the
[Sumas](/service-area/sumas/) and [Everson](/service-area/everson/) pages rather than
being generalised across the north.

If you are furnishing or refurnishing after a water event, screening second-hand and
donated furniture before it goes into a building is the cheapest intervention available.
That is written up at
[screening used furniture before it comes inside](/services/k9-bed-bug-detection/used-furniture-screening/).

## How reliable is a detection dog?

Reliable enough to be worth paying for, and variable enough that the team matters more
than the method.

The very high accuracy number that circulates in this industry comes from controlled
laboratory work. The field research, carried out in naturally infested apartments,
returned substantially lower average detection and a very wide spread between teams. Both
studies, the numbers and the citations are on our
[detection accuracy](/detection-accuracy/) page. I would rather point you there than
reshape a research finding into a sales sentence.

The spread is the useful part. It says the variable is the working team, not the species,
which is why we publish the handler, the dog, the certifying body and the certification
dates rather than a percentage. Until those fields are filled with something checkable, no
certification is claimed anywhere on this site. A credential you cannot verify is not a
credential, and this is the market where the difference shows.

How a search is actually run is described at
[how canine detection works](/how-canine-detection-works/).

## What we need from you before we arrive

Four things. Having them ready is most of the difference between a visit that works and a
day that gets written off.

**A unit list and a floor plan.** Not for tidiness. Unit size, layout and how many rooms
are furnished determine how long a proper search takes, and a price produced without them
is a guess wearing the costume of a quote.

**An access plan with a named person on it.** Who is admitting us, who holds keys, which
units have restricted access, and which residents need particular handling. On
agricultural housing the organisation controlling access is frequently not the owner, and
discovering that on the morning costs everybody the day.

**Notices served, with time in hand.** Building-wide work needs entry notice issued
properly under RCW 59.18.150. We book far enough ahead that this can be done correctly
rather than hurriedly.

**Honest occupancy information.** Clutter slows a search far more than floor area does. A
heavily occupied studio can take longer than a large sparse apartment. Describing a
building as lightly furnished when it is not does not make the day go faster. It moves the
overrun to the end of it, when the dog is tired and the searching is at its worst.

On that last point: a working dog has a limit, and we schedule to the limit rather than to
a daily unit target. A team pushed past its useful window produces precisely the
unreliable result the field research warns about, which would make the document worth less
than the paper. If the honest answer is that your property needs two days, we would rather
say so while quoting than discover it at four in the afternoon.
