## What does detection-only actually mean?

It means we have nothing to sell you at the end of the visit.

Most canine bed bug inspection in this state is performed by companies that also sell
treatment. The inspection is priced low, sometimes at nothing, because it is a route to
the treatment sale. That is not a criticism of any particular firm. It is a description of
how the transaction is structured, and everybody in the room understands it, including
your tenant, your board and their attorney.

Guardian is built the other way round. We apply no pesticides. We sell no treatment. We
take no referral fee from anyone who does. When we confirm a problem you engage a licensed
applicator of your own choosing, and we have no interest in which one you pick. When we
find nothing, that finding costs us the follow-on work we would have earned by finding
something, which is exactly why it is worth something to you.

The uncomfortable part of that arrangement is that we are a cost with no upsell behind it.
We would rather say so than pretend to compete on price with a loss leader.

## Who is this actually for?

Usually not the person with the bed bugs.

The people who call us are property managers, general managers, facilities directors,
housing directors and HOA boards. They are deciding who pays, what gets documented and how
a dispute gets closed. The person in the affected unit is rarely the one holding the
budget, and almost never the one who will need the paperwork eighteen months from now.

That shapes what we produce. The valuable thing is not a verbal all-clear at the door. It
is a dated, signed, third-party document that survives being read by somebody who was not
there and does not trust you. If you are a manager who has ever lost a chargeback dispute
you were factually right about, you already know the difference between knowing something
and being able to show it.

We work across [multifamily and property management](/commercial/multifamily/), hotels and
lodging, cruise and maritime, theatres and venues, student housing, senior living and HOA
grounds. The buyer differs in each, but the underlying purchase is the same: a defensible
record.

## What are the two services, and why these two?

Canine bed bug detection, and handler-directed deterrent work on resident Canada geese.
They look unrelated and they are not.

Both are jobs where a trained dog does something a person cannot do at a workable speed,
where the client needs a written record afterwards, and where the legal boundaries around
what a vendor may do are sharper than most vendors admit.

**Bed bug detection** covers inspections, verification after somebody else has treated,
turnover screening between tenancies, and building-wide sweeps. The [flagship

## What does the goose work involve?

A dog, a handler, a schedule, and a set of limits we publish before anyone asks.

Resident Canada geese settle on managed open ground because it suits them: mown grass to
graze, open sightlines to watch for threats, and water nearby. Corporate lawns, HOA common
areas, golf courses, school playing fields, park turf and retention ponds are close to
purpose-built for them. Once a flock has established a routine on a site, the droppings
become a walkway and liability problem, and the ground stops being usable for what it was
landscaped for.

The method is handler-directed hazing. The dog is worked deliberately, under control, to
make the site feel like ground that is patrolled rather than ground that is safe. There is
no contact, there is no injury, and nests and eggs are never disturbed. Washington
Department of Fish and Wildlife's own guidance names handler-directed dogs as the method of
choice for large open areas such as golf courses, airports, parks, agricultural fields and
corporate parks. The same guidance is equally clear that dogs can be the wrong choice in
residential areas, in continuously used parks, near roadways and on large water bodies, and
we take that half of it as seriously as the flattering half.

Three limits are worth stating up front, because they are where this work goes wrong.

**It is a programme, not a visit.** Geese come back to ground that stops being defended. A
single afternoon achieves a temporary result and a recurring invoice achieves a permanent
one. If somebody sells you a one-off as a solution, they are selling you a repeat.

**Nest and egg work is not ours to offer.** Registration under the federal Resident Canada
Goose Nest and Egg Depredation Order belongs to the landowner, the association or the local
government. A vendor cannot hold it. We could only ever act as an agent under somebody
else's registration, and we do not currently offer that.

**Hazing pauses during the flightless molt.** For a period each year the birds cannot fly,
and a bird that cannot leave cannot be moved humanely or defensibly. That pause is published
operating policy rather than a judgement call made on the day, and it is the sort of thing
you want in writing from a vendor before you sign rather than discovered in July.

Canada geese are federally protected under the Migratory Bird Treaty Act, and the federal
definition of "take" expressly includes pursuit. That is precisely why how the work is
conducted, and how it is documented, is a legal question rather than a presentational one.
For grounds managers the practical value is often the record itself: a dated programme log
is what lets a board demonstrate it acted reasonably and proportionately. The detail is at
[HOAs, golf and grounds](/commercial/hoas-and-grounds/).

## What is in the report?

The report is the product. Everything else is how the report gets made.

Every location the dog alerts on is recorded against the space and the position within it.
Next to each alert sits the visual confirmation result, in one of three states.
**Confirmed**, meaning live insects, cast skins, eggs or faecal staining were seen and
photographed. **Not confirmed**, meaning the dog alerted and nothing was visually found.
**Inconclusive**, meaning access or conditions prevented a proper look.

That middle state is the one that quietly disappears from other people's paperwork, and we
keep it. An unconfirmed alert is real information: it might be residual odour from a past
infestation, a scent source in an adjoining void, or the dog being wrong. Recording it
honestly is what makes the confirmed findings credible. A report in which every single
alert was confirmed should make you suspicious rather than reassured.

Where nothing is found, you get a written all-clear stating what was searched and what was
not. We keep the file so it can be reissued, because the request almost always arrives
months later and usually from somebody's lawyer. The standard we hold that document to is
described at
[what makes a report defensible](/compliance/what-a-defensible-report-contains/).

## How good is a detection dog, honestly?

Good enough to be worth paying for, and far more variable than the industry likes to say.

There is a very high accuracy figure that circulates in this market. It comes from
controlled laboratory work with planted hides. There is a second figure from field research
in naturally infested apartments, and it is a great deal lower with an enormous spread
between teams. Both are peer-reviewed. They are answering different questions, and only one
of them resembles your building.

We publish both numbers, both citations and the full explanation on the
[detection accuracy](/detection-accuracy/) page, and we deliberately do not repeat a bare
percentage anywhere else on this site. A figure detached from the conditions that produced
it is not information, it is decoration.

The spread is the part worth understanding. When results range across almost the entire
possible scale depending on which team was tested, the variable is the team, not the
species. A detection dog is not a specification you buy off a shelf. It is a working animal
whose performance depends on training maintenance, blind-trial discipline, handler reading
and how the search is run.

That is why we publish the handler, the dog, the certifying body and the certification
dates rather than a percentage. Where one of those fields is not yet filled in on this
site, nothing is claimed in its place. A credential you cannot check is not a credential,
and a placeholder that ships is worse than a blank.

## What does Washington law actually require?

Less than the marketing suggests in some places, and more than people expect in others.

**RCW 59.18.060(4)** puts a duty on landlords to provide a reasonable pest control
programme at the start of a tenancy and, other than in a single-family residence, to
control infestation during the tenancy except where the tenant caused it. **RCW 59.18.130**
is where tenant liability for extermination costs comes from. **RCW 59.18.150** governs the
entry notice you must give before inspecting a unit, and it is the provision that decides
whether a building-wide sweep is workable.

Local rules stack on top unevenly. Seattle names bed bugs explicitly and attaches
penalties running from $150 to $500 per day. Most of the state does not, and runs on the
state statute alone. A policy written for a Seattle portfolio therefore over-documents
elsewhere, and a policy written for a Whatcom portfolio under-documents the Seattle assets.

One thing that does not exist, despite being asserted regularly in trade articles: there is
no statewide statute in this state obliging a landlord to tell a prospective tenant about a
prior infestation. The bill usually cited concerns notice periods for rent increases and
has nothing to do with pests. We mention it because a vendor willing to invent a statute to
close a sale is probably willing to invent other things.

The plain-language summaries live at
[who is responsible under Washington landlord-tenant law](/compliance/washington-landlord-tenant-bed-bugs/),
[Seattle rental bed bug rules](/compliance/seattle-rental-bed-bug-rules/) and
[entry notice for unit inspections](/compliance/entry-notice-for-unit-inspections/).

## What will we not do?

The boundaries are not caveats bolted onto the service. They are the service.

We will not recommend a treatment method, a product or an active ingredient. That is
separately licensed work in Washington and we do not hold that licence. When you ask which
approach to use, the honest answer is that we are not the right people to ask and are not
permitted to advise you. Referral is always to a licensed applicator of your own choosing,
never to a named product and never to a particular company.

We will not promise elimination, because we do not perform it. Where the word guarantee is
used on this site it is a defined term with written conditions attached, set out at
[our guarantee](/our-guarantee/), and it covers our process rather than your outcome. Any
detection company offering a guaranteed result is selling past the published evidence.

We will not take nest or egg work on geese. Registration under the federal Resident Canada
Goose Nest and Egg Depredation Order belongs to the landowner, the association or the local
government, never to a vendor. And we pause hazing during the flightless molt, as published
operating policy rather than a case-by-case judgement, because birds that cannot fly cannot
be moved humanely.

We will not claim a certification before it is issued, invent a review, publish a client
count we do not have permission to use, or attach a number to something we have not
measured.
