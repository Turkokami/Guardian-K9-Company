## What makes Lynden a different job from Bellingham?

Twenty minutes of driving, and an almost completely different building stock.

Bellingham is a university city with dense older rental housing and a municipal rental
registration programme. Lynden is an agricultural city. There are relatively few large
conventional apartment complexes. What there is instead is family housing, a compact
historic downtown, church and school properties that carry a surprising share of the local
overnight and gathering capacity, and a seasonal agricultural workforce housed in
arrangements that look nothing like a lease.

That changes what we are asked to do. In Bellingham the common request is a unit or a small
building with a tenant complaint behind it. Here it is more often a whole facility before a
season, a bunkhouse arrangement, or an institutional property with a committee that wants
something in writing.

We work Lynden on the regular schedule rather than as an excursion. That is worth saying
plainly, because for a company based in the Puget Sound corridor this is the far end of a
long drive, and the behaviour that produces is predictable: long lead times and dates that
move when something closer comes up.

## Why is agricultural workforce housing scoped separately?

Because almost every assumption that holds for an apartment building fails here.

Whatcom County is one of the country's significant red raspberry producing areas, and the
labour that brings in is seasonal. The housing that supports it operates under its own
licensing, access and notice arrangements. Occupancy follows the crop calendar rather than a
lease year. The organisation that controls access is frequently not the organisation that
owns the building. Residents may not share a first language with the inspector. And the
practical consequences of a positive finding land on people with very little slack in their
circumstances.

None of that makes the work harder to do well. It makes it different to arrange, and the
arranging is where it goes wrong. We ask who is authorised to admit us, what the occupancy
pattern is that week, and whether interpretation is needed, before we ask about dates. A
search booked on conventional multifamily assumptions arrives to find half the spaces
inaccessible and produces a document that overstates its own coverage.

I will not claim expertise in the licensing framework itself, because that is somebody
else's specialism and pretending otherwise would be the sort of thing this site exists to
avoid. What I can say is how we handle it: scoped separately, quoted separately, and any
space we could not properly search recorded as inconclusive rather than clear.

## What governs a rental here?

State law, with no municipal layer on top.

**RCW 59.18.150** governs entry notice and requires at least two days written notice to
enter and inspect.

Lynden adds nothing naming bed bugs specifically. Neither does most of Washington. Bellingham
operates a rental registration and safety inspection programme under BMC 6.15, but that is
built around life and fire safety and does not extend here. Seattle is the outlier, with bed
bugs named explicitly and penalties running from $150 to $500 per day.

The consequence of having no local layer is not that less care is needed. It is that when
causation is disputed there is no municipal inspection finding to point at, so your own
dated record is the entirety of your evidence. That is an argument for keeping a good one.
The statutory position is set out at
[who is responsible under Washington landlord-tenant law](/compliance/washington-landlord-tenant-bed-bugs/).

## Does the border crossing change anything?

It changes the mix of who passes through, without changing what we do about it.

The Lynden–Aldergrove crossing brings steady cross-border movement through the city. For
lodging operators that means a share of guests who have travelled and stayed elsewhere
recently, which is the ordinary introduction pathway rather than an exotic one.

I am not going to put a number on that. I have not seen a dataset measuring introduction
rates by travel route in this county, and inventing one to make the point sound rigorous
would be exactly the behaviour the rest of this site argues against. What is defensible is
the general pattern: accommodation serving travellers carries more introduction opportunity
per room-night than housing serving settled residents, which is why lodging protocols exist
at all.

The practical version for an operator is to screen on a cadence rather than after a
complaint, because a complaint is the most expensive possible way to discover something. The
setting is covered at [hotels and lodging](/commercial/hotels-and-lodging/).

## When should the Fair and the fairgrounds change your plans?

Before and after, not during.

The Northwest Washington Fairgrounds pull very large visitor volume into a small city across
defined weeks. Every bed in the area is worth more during that period and no room can
comfortably be taken out of service. That is precisely when you least want to find something.

So the useful screening moments are the shoulder either side. Before the season, so anything
present is dealt with while a room can still be held. After it, so a heavy turnover period
does not roll straight into the rest of the year undetected. Operators who work that rhythm
spend less across a year than operators who respond to a complaint in the middle of the busy
weeks.

The same logic applies to the tulip season a county south, and it is written up on the
[Mount Vernon](/service-area/mount-vernon/) page. Seasonal concentration is one of the
genuine structural features of doing this work in the northern counties, and it is entirely
plannable.

## What about church, school and community properties?

They hold more of the overnight and gathering capacity here than the hotel sector does, and
they buy differently.

A church hosting groups, a school with residential or event use, a community hall with
regular gatherings: these are properties where many people pass through soft furnishings,
where nobody has a facilities department in the professional sense, and where the person
reading our report is a volunteer board member or an administrator rather than a property
professional.

Two things follow. The report has to be legible to somebody who does not do this for a
living, which is a writing problem rather than an inspection problem. And the scope
conversation has to be honest about what a single visit establishes, because these buyers
are more likely than commercial clients to hear "all clear" as "permanently fine."

We handle both by keeping the format identical to what a regional property manager receives:
what was searched, what was found, what was not searched, dated and signed. The standard is
described at
[what makes a report defensible](/compliance/what-a-defensible-report-contains/).

## Are the geese here worth a programme?

On open agricultural and institutional ground, frequently yes.

Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife's Living with Wildlife guidance names
handler-directed dogs as the method of choice for large open areas including agricultural
fields, parks and corporate grounds. Dairy pasture, berry operation margins, school playing
fields and the fairgrounds are all that shape. The same guidance is explicit that dogs can be
inappropriate in residential areas, in continuously used parks, near roadways and on large
water bodies, and we apply that half with the same seriousness.

What we do is handler-directed hazing of resident Canada geese: no contact, no injury, no
nests or eggs disturbed. Canada geese are federally protected under the Migratory Bird
Treaty Act and the federal definition of take at 50 CFR 10.12 includes pursuit, which is why
how the work is conducted and recorded is a legal question rather than a presentational one.

Two standing limits. Registration under the Resident Canada Goose Nest and Egg Depredation
Order at 50 CFR 21.162 belongs to the landowner, association or local government, never to a
vendor, so nest and egg work is not ours to offer. And hazing pauses during the flightless
molt, as published operating policy, because birds that cannot fly cannot be moved humanely.

One local clarification worth making, because it comes up constantly in this county and the
next: the enormous wintering flocks the valley is known for are **snow geese**, a different
population under a different management framework that WDFW addresses partly through hunting
programmes run with farmers. We do not offer snow goose work on cropland. Our goose service
is resident Canada geese on managed ground. Anyone blurring those two is worth questioning
closely. The legal picture is at
[Canada goose law in Washington](/compliance/canada-goose-law-washington/).

## What does the housing stock mean for an inspection?

Mixed vintage, which means the scoping assumption has to be made building by building.

The downtown core carries early-century construction with the trim, skirting voids and
wood-frame assemblies that offer far more harbourage than anything modern, and which take
materially longer to search properly. Residential development from the 1980s onward is
tighter, faster to clear per room, and offers fewer routes once something arrives.

Neither tells you anything about likelihood. Bed bugs travel with people and belongings, not
with buildings, and a new house takes them as readily as an old one. What building age
changes is the time and therefore the cost of a thorough search, which is a quoting question.

How full a room is beats both, and beats floor area. A heavily occupied room can take
longer than a large sparse one, and a space that cannot be searched properly is recorded as
inconclusive rather than clear. That word appearing in a report is unsatisfying on the day
and it is what keeps the rest of the document trustworthy.

## Does the time of year matter here?

More than in most of our service area, though not for the reason people assume.

Bed bugs live indoors in heated buildings, so there is no off-season during which a property
is safe and no month in which an inspection is pointless. Anyone describing this as seasonal
work is describing a different pest. What is genuinely seasonal in Lynden is everything
around the insect, and here that seasonality is unusually sharp.

The agricultural calendar drives occupancy in labour housing, concentrating arrivals and
departures into defined windows. The Fair concentrates visitor accommodation demand into
defined weeks. Cross-border leisure traffic rises and falls with the holiday pattern. And
the institutional properties that carry a lot of the local gathering capacity have their own
calendar of events and residential use.

Those four calendars do not line up with each other, which is the practical point. A single
annual inspection scheduled for administrative convenience in, say, February will miss the
moment that matters for three of them. The useful pattern is to screen each type of property
against its own rhythm: labour housing before and after the season, lodging on the shoulders
of the Fair, and institutional properties around their heaviest use.

None of that requires a bigger budget than an annual visit. It requires the visit to be
booked at the right point in the year, which is free.

## Working with the rest of the county

Lynden sits between the anchor market and the border, so it combines naturally with the
towns around it.

[Bellingham](/service-area/bellingham/) is the largest rental market in the north and where
the schedule is densest. [Ferndale](/service-area/ferndale/) runs on Cherry Point industrial
employment and contractor lodging that spikes around plant turnarounds.
[Blaine](/service-area/blaine/) carries the border crossings and a vacation-rental base at
Semiahmoo and Birch Bay. And [Everson](/service-area/everson/),
[Nooksack](/service-area/nooksack/) and [Sumas](/service-area/sumas/) are still shaped by
the November 2021 Nooksack flooding, which damaged the overwhelming majority of structures
in Sumas and displaced hundreds across the north county. Displacement, temporary housing and
replacement furniture are documented introduction pathways, and screening incoming
second-hand furnishings is the cheapest response available.

If you hold property across several of those, one schedule and one document format is worth
more than the sum of separate visits, and it is easier to deliver inside Whatcom than
anywhere else we work. The programme argument is at
[multifamily and property management](/commercial/multifamily/).

## What we will not do here


And we will not tell you a single visit settles a property permanently. It establishes what
was there on a date. For most operators of any size the useful thing is a cadence, and if
the honest answer is that you do not need one yet, that is the answer you will get. The full
map, including where we are slower, is at [where we work](/service-area/).

One point specific to this community, and it matters more here than the technical detail
above. A finding in workforce or congregate housing lands on people who cannot easily absorb
the disruption, and the temptation to handle it quietly is real. Handling it quietly is what
turns a contained problem into a building-wide one, because the units around it never get
looked at. A dated record naming what was searched and what was found protects the people
living there as much as it protects the owner, and it is the reason we write down the units
we could not enter rather than leaving them off the list.
